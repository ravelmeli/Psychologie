## Description

You find $100 in a jacket: a little smile, and the day goes on. You lose $100: the whole day is ruined. Yet it's exactly the same amount.

This reflex has a name: loss aversion. To your brain, a loss weighs about twice as much as an equal gain, on average. Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky illustrated it with a simple coin flip: to accept risking $100, most people need the chance of winning about $200.

It gets expensive: we hold on to a collapsing stock hoping to get back to our purchase price, we sell winners too early for fear of losing the gain, and retailers play on it with lines like "Only two left in stock" and "Offer ends tonight."

To take back control: wait 24 hours before any purchase made under pressure, ask yourself whether you'd buy it today if you didn't already own it, and set your rules in advance (budget, selling price) when you're calm.

And you, which loss has already pushed you into a bad decision?
