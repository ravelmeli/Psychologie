# Voice-over script — The test: $100 today or $110 in a month? (EN)

Target length: about 93 s (minimum 80 s). Amounts in dollars (channel convention). 9 paragraphs.
Suggested pauses: 3 seconds of silence after paragraph 1 and after paragraph 4 (time to answer).

---

Quick test. Answer without thinking: a hundred dollars today, or a hundred and ten dollars in a month?

Whatever you chose, what comes next may surprise you.

Waiting a month to earn ten dollars is a ten percent return in one month. Repeated every month, that would be over two hundred percent a year. No bank offers you that.

Second question. A hundred dollars in twelve months, or a hundred and ten dollars in thirteen months?

Now most people pick the hundred and ten. Yet it's exactly the same wait: one month for ten dollars more. Only the word "now" has disappeared.

This reflex is called present bias. As soon as a reward is immediate, it weighs much more in your brain than the same reward later. Economists have observed it since the nineteen-eighties.

And it gets expensive. It's what pushes saving off until tomorrow, what makes us buy now and pay later, what empties an account for a whim. Today's you is negotiating against the you of thirty years from now, and it's always today's you who is in the room.

So how do you get around it? First, automate: a transfer to savings on payday, before the present gets a vote. Then, decide in advance: commit to saving part of your next raise. Finally, give that money a purpose: travel, security, freedom. A concrete future weighs more than a vague one.

So what did you choose at the start? Write 100 or 110 in the comments, and tell us if you changed your mind on the second question.

---

To verify before publishing:
- "Over two hundred percent a year" assumes the gain is reinvested every month (110% monthly growth gives about +214% over 12 months).
- "Most people pick the hundred and ten" on the second question is the classic result (preference reversal, Thaler 1981), but the proportions vary with the amounts and the sample.
