## Description

You're told to quit your coffee to get rich. Let's do the math: a $4 coffee, 20 days a month, is $80. On a $3,000 salary, that's under 3% of your budget (invented example, for illustration).

Housing, transportation and food often make up more than half of a household's spending. In our example: $1,000 of rent, $450 for transportation, $500 for groceries. The rent alone is more than twelve times the coffee budget.

So why is this myth so widespread? Because the coffee is visible, easy to count and easy to cut, while insurance, your phone plan or your energy contract renew once a year without a second thought. Our brain focuses on what's small and daily. And the myth has a hidden cost: guilt, which keeps you away from the real levers.

Where to act: compare or renegotiate your big categories once a year (insurance, phone plan, energy, bank), automate your savings so you stop relying on willpower, and keep the pleasures that truly make you happy while cutting the automatic reflexes. Small repeated expenses do add up, but they aren't the priority.
