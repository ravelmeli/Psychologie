## Description

Pulling out a 20-euro bill for a coffee stings. Swiping a card barely registers. This reflex has a name: the pain of paying. With cash, you see your money leave and your brain registers the loss. With a card, the transaction becomes invisible: one tap, one beep, done.

A study at MIT found that people are willing to pay more when they pay by card. It isn't a lack of willpower: when money doesn't feel real, the brain simplifies. And retailers know it, from one-click payments to automatic subscriptions and buy now, pay later.

The trap is the bill that arrives later, on your monthly statement. To take back control, try paying in cash for a week, or withdrawing a fixed amount every Monday: make the pain visible.

And you, which expense hurts the most?
