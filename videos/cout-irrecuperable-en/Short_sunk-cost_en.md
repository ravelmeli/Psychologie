## Description

A $40-a-month subscription you barely use. You keep it "because you already paid." What if that's a trap in your brain?

It's the sunk cost fallacy: a past expense you can't get back whatever you decide still weighs on your choices. The brain hates losing, so it keeps going to "get its money's worth." Researchers Hal Arkes and Catherine Blumer studied it in the 1980s, using theater tickets: people who paid full price attended more performances than those who got a discount, for the same enjoyment.

It's everywhere: finishing a meal you don't like, watching a boring movie to the end, keeping a useless subscription, continuing a project that isn't working. The more you've invested, the harder it is to stop.

How do you get out? 1. Ask the right question: "If I hadn't paid anything, would I do this today?" If the answer is no, stop. 2. Count the future cost: every extra month is $40 more. The past is gone, but you can still save the future. 3. Turn the loss into a lesson: what you paid is the price of a lesson.
