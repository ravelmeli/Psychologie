## Description

You earn more than you did five years ago. So why has your bank account barely moved?

Take an example (invented, for illustration): you earn $2,000 a month, spend $1,900 and save $100. Your salary goes up to $3,000. A year later, you spend $2,900, and you're still saving $100. This is lifestyle inflation: often, every raise becomes the new normal and spending rises to meet it.

Behind it is hedonic adaptation: our brain gets used to improvements fast, and yesterday's luxury becomes today's minimum. Psychologists call it the hedonic treadmill: you keep running, but you stay in the same place. The trap is that each upgrade looks reasonable (a slightly pricier rent, one more subscription, one more trip), but together they swallow the entire raise.

To get out of it: look at your savings rate instead of your salary, put half of every raise aside before your lifestyle adjusts, and choose one or two upgrades that truly make you happier.
