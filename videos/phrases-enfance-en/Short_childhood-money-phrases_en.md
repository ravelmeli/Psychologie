## Description

"Money doesn't grow on trees." You've probably heard that sentence as a kid. And it may still be steering your choices today.

Psychologist Brad Klontz writes about "money scripts": beliefs about money learned early, rarely chosen. "We can't afford it," "Rich people are dishonest," "We don't talk about money at the table," "Money can't buy happiness": repeated dozens of times, never discussed.

As adults, they turn into reflexes: avoiding looking at your account, feeling guilty spending even when you can afford it, distrusting success, including your own. The trap is that you don't see them: they sound like common sense.

How do you free yourself? 1. Spot the phrase: when you hesitate to spend, to ask for a raise or to look at your account, ask yourself what sentence you hear in your head. 2. Find where it comes from: who said it, in what situation, what reality it was protecting someone from. 3. Rewrite it to make it true today, for example: "Money gets built, and I'm allowed to enjoy it."

And you, which money phrase did you hear at home? Write it in the comments.
